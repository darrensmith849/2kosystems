## Recognition section

**Eyebrow:** 01 — WHERE IT BREAKS

**H2:**

> The work is happening. The process is making it harder than it should be.

**Lead:**

> Most operational problems do not announce themselves as “we need software”. They show up as waiting, rework, missing information and controls that depend on somebody remembering.

### Approvals wait in inboxes

The request exists, but there is no shared queue, visible owner or automatic escalation. Progress depends on somebody chasing it.

### Reports are assembled, not produced

The same information is copied from the same sources every week or month, with a fresh opportunity for delay and error.

### Everyone has a different version

The process lives across spreadsheets, email, paper and WhatsApp. Each tool contains part of the truth and nobody owns the whole record.

### Exceptions arrive after the cost

A threshold was crossed days ago, but the issue becomes visible only when somebody opens a report or a customer complains.

### Controls depend on memory

The procedure says a check must happen. Nothing in the process prevents the work from moving forward when it does not.

### The improvement quietly decays

The project closed and performance improved. Six months later the manual control has weakened and the old workarounds are back.

**Closing line:**

> These are rarely separate technology problems. They are signs that the process, capability and control no longer fit together.

## When to use it

**H2:**

> You can see the symptoms. You cannot yet name the constraint.

Use a Process Review when:

- people describe the same process differently;
- the work moves through several disconnected tools;
- waiting and chasing appear larger than the work itself;
- a control depends on a checklist, spreadsheet or supervisor memory;
- the team is asking for technology before the process is understood;
- an earlier improvement is beginning to decay.

## What happens

### Before

We agree the process, site, participants and explicit exclusions. You provide the actual artifacts used to run the work—not a presentation about how it should run.

### During

We walk the process with somebody who performs it and somebody who owns the outcome. We record the handoffs, tools, waiting, workarounds and control method.

### After

You receive a three-to-four-page memo naming the principal failure, its likely impact and the smallest appropriate next step.

## What the recommendation may say

- improve the process before doing anything else;
- train or coach the team;
- automate a bounded workflow;
- replace or build a system;
- investigate through a fuller audit;
- use an existing off-the-shelf product;
- do not build anything.

## Boundaries

One process, one site and up to four primary participants. The review does not include a detailed financial model, technical architecture, prototype or implementation scope.

## What should be automated

Automation is usually justified when the work is:

- repeated frequently;
- governed by stable rules;
- delayed by handoffs or chasing;
- re-entered across systems;
- vulnerable to skipped checks;
- measurable through clear events;
- expensive enough to justify intervention.

## What should not be automated yet

Do not automate when:

- people do not agree on the process;
- the rule changes every time;
- the real constraint is authority or capability;
- the source data cannot be trusted;
- the decision carries consequences that require accountable human judgement;
- the volume and value do not justify the operating cost.

## Rules-based automation

### Route and approve

Move requests through delegation rules, thresholds and escalation without relying on copied email chains.

### Capture and validate

Create the record where the work happens and reject impossible or incomplete information before it reaches the report.

### Reconcile and report

Compare sources, surface exceptions and produce recurring reporting without rebuilding the same pack.

### Integrate and notify

Move verified information between existing systems and alert the person who can act when a threshold is crossed.

## Intelligence-assisted work

### Extract and classify

Read documents or messages, identify the relevant information and place it into the correct workflow for verification.

### Triage and summarise

Prioritise work, assemble context and reduce the time people spend searching before making a decision.

### Retrieve and draft

Bring procedures and source material to the point of work and prepare a response or record for accountable review.

### Converse where conversation is the process

Handle bounded enquiries, bookings, follow-ups or status interactions with explicit escalation and outcome tracking.

## Human decision boundary

**H2:**

> Consequential decisions keep a named person.

Automation may recommend, assemble, route and record. Safety, employment, medical, legal, credit and other consequential decisions retain the required human authority and audit trail.

# Shared FAQ copy

## How do you decide whether the answer is process, training or technology?

We begin with how the work runs today, the observable failure and the measure that matters. If the constraint is an unnecessary step or unclear ownership, we improve the process. If performance depends on capability or judgement, we involve the training division. If repetitive handling or a manual control is the constraint, we automate or systemise it. Many worthwhile interventions contain more than one of these.

## Do we have to start with a Process Review?

No. If the failure is visible in an existing artifact—a spreadsheet that has outgrown itself, a known job-card process or a clearly bounded approval chain—a free Fit Call may be enough to select a fixed-scope product or pilot. When people disagree on how the process works, the review is the appropriate start.

## Is this consulting or software development?

It is operational improvement delivered through the intervention the process requires. 2KO Systems specialises in automation and operational software, but the wider 2KO Group also provides practitioner-led improvement, training and an improvement-programme platform.

## Do you use AI?

Where it measurably reduces handling or improves access to information. Typical uses include extraction, classification, triage, summarisation, knowledge retrieval and assisted drafting. We do not use AI as a substitute for a clear process, reliable data or accountable human decisions.

## Can we operate the system without 2KO?

Yes. Commissioned source, documentation and data are yours under the agreed contract, and the system is designed to be operated independently. Managed services are optional.

## What happens if the audit says not to build anything?

That is a valid result. An audit that could only recommend buying software from us would not be worth commissioning.
