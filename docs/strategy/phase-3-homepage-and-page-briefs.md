# 2KO Systems — Phase 3 Homepage and Page Briefs

Status: Message architecture; not final production copy  
Date: 12 September 2026

## 1. Message hierarchy

### Category

Process improvement, operational automation and managed improvement.

### Primary promise

> **Improve the process. Automate what should never fail.**

### Explanation

> 2KO finds operational friction, improves the workflow and builds the systems that keep the gain from slipping backwards. When the constraint is capability, we train. When it is repetition, we automate. When it is both, we do both.

### Differentiator

> Process-improvement people who build and operate software.

### Operating principle

> Automate where the rules repeat. Train where judgement matters. Measure both.

### Ongoing-service promise

> We do not only keep the software running. We keep the process improving.

### Primary CTA

> Bring us the process

## 2. Homepage structure

### Section 1 — Hero: begin with the operational outcome

**Purpose:** Establish that 2KO improves processes and uses training, automation and systems as interventions.

**Recommended headline:**

> Improve the process. Automate what should never fail.

**Lead:**

> 2KO identifies operational friction, improves the workflow and builds the controls that stop the gain from slipping backwards. Process, training and technology—selected by the problem, not prescribed in advance.

**Primary CTA:** Bring us the process.  
**Secondary CTA:** See how improvement works.

**Hero proof:** Use one verified result when available. Until then, use a factual operating statement such as fixed scope, measurable baseline and source ownership—not an invented performance statistic.

### Section 2 — Recognition: show the problems buyers already feel

**Purpose:** Allow the visitor to recognise their operation before introducing services.

Use six symptoms:

- approvals wait in individual inboxes;
- reports take days to assemble;
- work is re-entered across spreadsheets and messages;
- exceptions are found after the cost has occurred;
- controls depend on someone remembering;
- improvements fade when the project team moves on.

Close with:

> These are rarely separate software problems. They are signs that the process, capability and control no longer fit together.

### Section 3 — Intervention decision

**Purpose:** Prove that 2KO does not prescribe software indiscriminately.

Show four possible outcomes:

1. Improve the process.
2. Equip the people.
3. Automate the repetitive work.
4. Systemise the control.

Add:

> Sometimes the honest answer is that nothing should be built.

### Section 4 — The improvement lifecycle

**Purpose:** Explain the group advantage and connect to DMAIC.

Show:

> Diagnose → Measure → Improve → Train → Automate → Systemise → Sustain

For each step, describe a client outcome rather than an internal activity.

### Section 5 — Demonstration: the improved control in operation

**Purpose:** Retain the existing visual and technical strength while connecting it to process performance.

Reframe the interactive console around:

- failure before the intervention;
- improved process rule;
- automated routing and escalation;
- human decision point;
- audit trail;
- metric the system would measure.

Keep the “illustrative records” label. Do not display invented uptime or savings as though they were proof.

### Section 6 — Systems and automation

**Purpose:** Explain the delivery capability after the process story is clear.

Two categories:

- **Rules-based automation:** routing, validation, escalation, reconciliation, reporting and integrations.
- **Intelligence-assisted work:** extraction, classification, triage, summarisation, knowledge and assisted drafting.

Close with:

> Judgement stays with your people. Repetitive handling and routine control move into the system.

**CTA:** Explore systems and automation.

### Section 7 — Proof

**Purpose:** Replace logo-led credibility with evidence-led credibility.

Until verified client cases exist:

- publish the approved Sigmafy platform-scale story;
- use one transparent process-audit example labelled fictional only as an explanation of method, not a result;
- label the client strip as organisations served across the 2KO Group;
- explain the observed/reported/measured evidence standard.

When evidence is ready, show no more than three case studies on the homepage, each with:

- baseline;
- intervention;
- result;
- sustained period;
- evidence classification.

**CTA:** See results on record.

### Section 8 — Managed improvement

**Purpose:** Make the ongoing relationship part of the main offer rather than a pricing-page afterthought.

Headline:

> The system is live. The next constraint has already moved.

Explain:

> Care keeps the technology reliable. Managed Improvement keeps one system-enabled process measurable and improving. Continuous Improvement Partner puts an important workstream onto a recurring performance rhythm.

**CTA:** Compare managed improvement options.

### Section 9 — Engagement ladder

**Purpose:** Show the smallest appropriate starting point and preserve transparent pricing.

Display:

- Process Review;
- Process and Automation Opportunity Audit;
- Workflow Automation Pilot;
- Operational System Build;
- Managed Improvement.

Do not mix website packages into this ladder.

### Section 10 — Group advantage and final CTA

**Purpose:** Connect Systems to training and Sigmafy without diluting its specialist role.

Message:

> One group can diagnose the process, build the capability, embed the control and sustain the improvement.

Final headline:

> Bring us the process that keeps going wrong.

## 3. Page brief — Process Review

**Job:** Convert buyers who see symptoms but cannot confidently name the cause.

**Required content:**

- who the review is for;
- what happens during the half day;
- artifacts and people required;
- deliverable example;
- possible recommendations, including training and do not build;
- price and credit terms;
- boundaries;
- questions procurement or sponsors ask.

**Primary CTA:** Book a Process Review.  
**Required proof:** Redacted example memo or clearly fictional format sample.

## 4. Page brief — Process and Automation Audit

**Job:** Sell evidence and prioritisation rather than discovery time.

**Required content:**

- difference between review and audit;
- fieldwork method;
- observed versus reported evidence;
- benefit calculation method;
- classification of intervention;
- standard versus extended scope;
- one-recommendation rule;
- example executive-summary format;
- price, timebox and credit terms.

**Primary CTA:** Quantify the opportunity.  
**Required proof:** Audit template and one permissioned or fictional worked structure, labelled accurately.

## 5. Page brief — Systems and Automation

**Job:** Demonstrate that 2KO can embed improved processes in dependable technology.

**Required content:**

- process-first statement;
- rules-based automation;
- intelligence-assisted automation;
- approvals, capture, escalation, reporting, portals and integrations;
- human-decision boundary;
- security, ownership and handover;
- productised systems;
- pilot route;
- relevant results.

**Primary CTA:** Prove one workflow.  
**Required proof:** Technical mechanism demo plus verified implementation case when available.

## 6. Page brief — Automation

**Job:** Capture automation demand without allowing AI to become a vague category.

**Required content:**

- symptoms that justify automation;
- when not to automate;
- rules-based versus intelligence-assisted work;
- examples by workstream;
- data and integration requirements;
- human escalation;
- pilot boundaries;
- ongoing optimisation route.

**Primary CTA:** Assess an automation opportunity.  
**Required proof:** At least one measured automation case before making performance claims.

## 7. Page brief — Managed Improvement

**Job:** Establish recurring process performance as a distinct service.

**Required content:**

- difference between Care and improvement;
- monthly improvement cycle;
- scorecard example;
- Care, Managed Improvement and CI Partner comparison;
- inclusions and exclusions;
- governance cadence;
- capacity and rollover rules;
- outcome-pricing conditions;
- exit and ownership terms;
- pricing once approved.

**Primary CTA:** Put one process on an improvement rhythm.  
**Required proof:** Three-month managed-improvement case before outcome-led claims are used.

## 8. Page brief — Method

**Job:** Explain how 2KO chooses and delivers the intervention.

**Required content:**

- Diagnose, Measure, Improve, Train, Automate, Systemise, Sustain;
- mapping to DMAIC;
- entry points for known versus unclear problems;
- baseline and success criteria;
- working software during pilots;
- decision gates and stop options;
- evidence and benefits review;
- connection to the relevant offer at each stage.

**Primary CTA:** Find your starting point.  
**Required proof:** Method artifacts and decision gates, not generic methodology claims.

## 9. Page brief — Results

**Job:** Become the site's main credibility destination.

**Required content:**

- verified case-study cards;
- filter by sector, process and intervention;
- baseline, result and sustained period visible before click-through;
- evidence labels;
- methodology for calculations;
- Sigmafy internal platform story;
- explicit treatment of anonymised clients.

**Primary CTA:** Discuss a similar process.  
**Publication rule:** No illustrative numerical outcome may appear as client proof.

## 10. Page brief — Sectors

**Job:** Translate the same process mechanisms into sector-specific language.

Each sector section should show:

- costly process failures;
- measures buyers recognise;
- likely process and capability causes;
- typical automation or system controls;
- one relevant proof item;
- appropriate first engagement.

Avoid implying engineering or physical-process expertise that 2KO does not provide.

**Primary CTA:** Review a process in this sector.

## 11. Page brief — Pricing

**Job:** Preserve transparency while distinguishing diagnostics, builds and managed improvement.

Structure:

1. diagnostic offers;
2. fixed-scope products;
3. pilot and build phases;
4. managed improvement;
5. commercial terms;
6. outcome-pricing explanation;
7. common questions.

Do not place website prices inside the operational-system ladder.

**Primary CTA:** Find your starting point.

## 12. Page brief — About

**Job:** Explain why 2KO understands process before technology.

Required content:

- process-improvement and training heritage;
- Systems division role;
- group model;
- delivery principles;
- boundaries and what 2KO does not do;
- ownership and no-lock-in position;
- registered-company and procurement information;
- real team and relevant delivery experience when available.

**Primary CTA:** Bring us the process.

## 13. Page brief — Contact and starting-point journey

**Job:** Capture enough information to route the enquiry without forcing the buyer to design the solution.

Ask:

- which process is involved;
- what keeps going wrong;
- frequency or volume;
- current consequence;
- how work is handled today;
- whether a baseline exists;
- who owns the process;
- site or region;
- contact information.

Do not ask the buyer to choose between training, AI, automation or a system before describing the problem.

Include a warning not to submit confidential operational data, personal information or regulated records through the public form.

**Primary CTA:** Send the process problem.

## 14. Page brief — Productised systems

**Job:** Retain high-intent conversion while connecting products to improvement outcomes.

Add to every product page:

- baseline measure captured before go-live;
- likely process constraint;
- control mechanism;
- post-launch measure;
- Managed Improvement route;
- accurate evidence status.

Avoid absolute outcome language where the outcome has not been measured.

## 15. Page brief — Websites

**Job:** Continue converting website demand without defining the operational-systems brand.

Requirements:

- remain directly accessible and indexable;
- retain separate pricing and conversion journey;
- remove from primary operational navigation;
- use a distinct visual or verbal label indicating another 2KO service;
- avoid placing website entry prices beside enterprise systems pricing;
- measure revenue and lead quality before deciding on a separate brand.
