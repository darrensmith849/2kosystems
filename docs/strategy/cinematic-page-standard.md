# 2KO Systems Cinematic Page Standard

## Purpose

This standard applies to every commercial page added to or materially redesigned on the 2KO Systems website. It protects the visual character approved on the process-optimisation homepage while keeping evidence, accessibility and conversion clarity intact.

Utility pages such as privacy and terms may remain deliberately quiet.

## Required page rhythm

Every commercial page should contain:

1. A strong first viewport with a clear proposition, one primary action and a dominant page-specific visual. The visual must be visible on arrival—not deferred until the visitor scrolls past the proposition and commercial facts.
2. An operational artefact, working demonstration or meaningful diagram near the hero.
3. At least one full-bleed visual or equivalent high-impact set piece.
4. A visible sequence, decision or mechanism that explains how the work changes.
5. A decisive closing section that reconnects the visual story to the next action.

No page should rely on more than two consecutive conventional text or card-grid sections.

## Composition rules

- Alternate scale, density and alignment rather than repeating centred headings over equal cards.
- Treat the hero as a visual proof surface. On product and service pages, the operational interface, artefact or mechanism should occupy at least as much visual importance as the headline at desktop sizes and enter the first viewport on mobile.
- Default to a top-to-bottom hero hierarchy: a minimal page identity, then a dominant full-width visual, then the main proposition and explanatory copy. Do not reduce the product scene to one side of a conventional copy-and-image split unless the subject genuinely requires it.
- Use large type to make a point, not merely to decorate a section.
- Prefer process lines, artefacts, instruments, evidence records and photographic plates to generic icon grids.
- Make the intervention visible: a queue routing, a control holding, evidence accumulating or a process moving.
- Reuse the colour system, typography, cinematic image grade and restrained reveal motion.
- Let each service page have its own visual job; consistency should not become sameness.

## Evidence rules

- Invented records, calculations and operational readouts must be labelled illustrative.
- Demonstrations must not be presented as client results.
- Observed, reported and calculated evidence must remain visibly distinct.
- Visual drama may increase confidence, but it may not overstate proof.

## Mobile standard

- The proposition, primary action and first artefact must remain legible at 360 pixels wide.
- Decorative layers must never create horizontal overflow.
- Large diagrams must recompose, not merely shrink.
- Fixed controls must not prevent access to primary content or actions.
- Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Review gate

Before a commercial page is accepted:

- inspect the first viewport at desktop and mobile sizes;
- inspect at least one mid-page visual transition;
- confirm the final action remains clear;
- check horizontal overflow;
- check keyboard and navigation behaviour;
- check the browser console;
- run source lint and a production build;
- verify all illustrative content is labelled.

## Page-specific visual grammar

Shared typography, navigation, colour discipline and interaction quality do not require every page to share the same composition. Each major offer or destination should choose a visual metaphor native to its subject and build the page around it.

- Reuse foundations, not entire stories.
- Avoid repeating the same hero instrument, photo plate and card grid on adjacent pages.
- Give each page at least one dominant bespoke environment: a process room, evidence ledger, operating cadence, workflow trace or other subject-specific interface.
- Let page rhythm, scale and density change when the content benefits, while preserving accessibility and brand restraint.
- Product and service pages must include at least one believable operational scene at meaningful scale. A buyer should be able to picture their records, roles and decisions inside it; decorative dashboards and context-free metrics do not meet this standard.
- Interface scenes must show the work being done—capture, validation, routing, approval, exception handling, reporting or evidence—not merely imply that software exists.
