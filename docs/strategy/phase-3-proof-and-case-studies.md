# 2KO Systems — Phase 3 Proof and Case-Study Plan

Status: Evidence inventory and case-study production brief  
Date: 12 September 2026

## 1. Evidence conclusion

The current public material does not yet contain enough verified outcome evidence to support a strong process-optimisation position.

The 2KO Group case-study page explicitly describes its examples as **illustrative, anonymised outcomes**. Those figures may inspire the shape of future case studies, but they must not be repeated on 2KO Systems as evidence of delivered results unless the original records and client permissions are recovered and verified.

The current 2KO Systems demonstrations are also explicitly invented. They prove design and technical capability, not client performance.

The proof programme must therefore do two things in parallel:

1. use the strongest truthful evidence already available;
2. create a repeatable process for producing verified outcome evidence from every future pilot and retainer.

## 3. Proof hierarchy

Use proof in this order of strength:

1. **Named, measured client outcome with permission.**
2. **Anonymised, measured client outcome with permission.**
3. **Measured internal product or operating result.**
4. **Observed process evidence without a financial claim.**
5. **Mechanism demonstration clearly labelled illustrative.**
6. **Group heritage or relationship evidence accurately attributed.**

Do not allow logos or interface demonstrations to substitute for outcome proof.

## 4. Selected case-study candidates

The following four candidates give the future site the most balanced proof set.

### Candidate 1 — Sigmafy: systemising improvement at platform scale

**Role in the story:** Demonstrates that 2KO can build and operate software around real improvement workflows rather than generic administration.

**Evidence currently available:**

- 342 companies represented on the platform;
- 59 companies linked to at least one project;
- 239 non-preview projects;
- 2,428 learner-written project solutions;
- approximately 6.3 million characters of project material;
- 96% of recorded solutions created in 2026 at the date of the audit.

**Important limitation:** These counts were measured on 20 August 2026 and must be refreshed. They show usage and scale, not client ROI or improvement outcomes.

**Evidence required before publication:**

- fresh aggregate counts from production;
- written confirmation that the figures may be published;
- confirmation of how companies are counted;
- privacy review ensuring no project, learner or company content is exposed;
- clear statement that Sigmafy is a 2KO Group platform rather than a third-party client implementation.

**Recommended headline direction:**

> How 2KO turned improvement projects, cohorts and controls into one operating platform.

### Candidate 2 — A verified systemisation project

**Role in the story:** Proves the core Systems promise: an operational process moved from disconnected manual tools into a controlled workflow.

**Preferred project profile:**

- approval, job-card, contractor, incident or reporting workflow;
- live for at least 90 days;
- transaction log available;
- identifiable before-state artifact such as a spreadsheet, form or message thread;
- sponsor and process owner willing to validate the account;
- at least one outcome metric and one control metric.

**Preferred measures:**

- elapsed cycle time;
- avoidable handling time;
- overdue or unowned work;
- data completeness;
- rework or duplicate capture;
- reporting preparation time;
- percentage of work using the intended process.

**Evidence required:**

- baseline and comparison periods;
- data source;
- process map before and after;
- calculation sheet;
- client approval of wording;
- explanation of other contributing changes.

**Recommended headline pattern:**

> From [manual failure] to [controlled result]: how one [workflow] changed in [period].

### Candidate 3 — Train, Improve and Embed

**Role in the story:** Proves the unique cross-group advantage: training develops capability, a live project delivers an improvement, and a system embeds the control.

**Preferred source:** A completed Six Sigma project whose control method is still manual, or a future cohort where systemisation can be agreed from the start.

**Evidence required:**

- sponsor consent and contractual permission;
- original problem and goal statements;
- verified baseline and outcome;
- completed training and project evidence;
- control plan before systemisation;
- reason automation was appropriate;
- sustained measure at 90 days or later;
- separation of the learner project benefit from the later systems benefit.

**Privacy constraint:** Learner submissions were collected for training and certification. They must not be mined for sales or public stories without an appropriate lawful basis and permission. The safest route is a sponsor review of the organisation's own projects followed by explicit participation.

**Recommended headline direction:**

> The team improved the process. The system made the new standard difficult to bypass.

### Candidate 4 — Managed Improvement pilot

**Role in the story:** Proves that the proposed retainer is more than hosting or prepaid development time.

**Preferred client profile:**

- existing production system;
- engaged process owner;
- stable enough to improve rather than rescue;
- available transaction history;
- known backlog;
- willingness to run a three-month trial and approve an anonymised account.

**Three-month evidence plan:**

- establish performance and control baseline;
- conduct three monthly improvement cycles;
- record each constraint selected;
- record intervention type: process, capability, system or combination;
- measure change and actual delivery capacity;
- complete a quarterly benefits review;
- obtain client assessment of the operating cadence.

**Recommended headline direction:**

> Three months, three constraints, one continuously improving workflow.

## 5. Reserve case-study candidate — Get Off Excel

Minimum proof:

## 6. Case-study selection scorecard

Score each candidate from 0 to 2.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | None | Reconstructable | Documented before intervention |
| Outcome data | Anecdotal | Partial | System or finance evidence |
| Attribution | Highly shared | Mixed | Mechanism substantially attributable |
| Sustained period | No follow-up | Under 90 days | 90 days or longer |
| Client permission | Unavailable | Possible | Confirmed |
| Named publication | No | Anonymised only | Named use approved |
| Buyer relevance | Peripheral | Useful | Directly matches target process |
| Cross-group story | None | One adjacent division | Training, improvement and systemisation |
| Visual evidence | None | Screenshots only | Before/after artifacts and process map |
| Financial credibility | Unsupported | Estimated | Client or finance validated |

Interpretation:

- **0–7:** do not publish;
- **8–13:** mechanism story only, without strong outcome headline;
- **14–17:** publishable anonymised case after review;
- **18–20:** flagship proof candidate.

## 7. Standard published case-study structure

Every case study will use the same sequence.

1. **The process** — name the bounded workstream.
2. **The baseline** — show how it performed and over what period.
3. **Where it broke** — explain the operational mechanism.
4. **What 2KO changed** — distinguish process, training, automation and system work.
5. **The result** — give the observed measure and comparison period.
6. **What made it hold** — identify the control and operating ownership.
7. **What remains** — state limitations, next constraint or remaining manual work.
8. **Evidence note** — state whether figures are measured, calculated or client-reported.

## 8. Case-study evidence pack

No draft begins until the evidence pack contains:

- signed or recorded publication permission;
- approved naming level: named, anonymised, or internal only;
- scope and dates;
- baseline dataset or artifact;
- comparison dataset;
- metric definitions;
- calculation workbook or record;
- interview with process owner;
- interview with one person performing the work;
- before and after process maps;
- screenshots or photographs cleared for publication;
- dependency and attribution note;
- final client wording approval.
