# 2KO Systems — Phase 2 Pricing and Validation Model

Status: Internal working model; not approved for publication  
Date: 12 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

Test whether the proposed retainer promise can be delivered profitably and define the evidence required before prices are published.

This is not an accounting forecast. It is a transparent commercial model built from currently visible assumptions in the repository.

## 2. Known public inputs

| Input | Current value |
| --- | ---: |
| Published day rate | R9,500 |
| Published hourly rate | R1,250 |
| Care | R7,500/month |
| Improve | R18,500/month |
| Partner | R38,500/month |
| Minimum retainer term | 6 months |
| Annual prepayment discount | 10% |
| Annual escalation | CPI + 2% |
| Third-party pass-through margin | 15% |
| Current stated fully loaded delivery cost assumption | approximately R4,000/day |

The R4,000/day figure appears in internal pricing commentary and must be validated against current payroll, contractor, management and operating costs.

## 3. Commercial assumptions for modelling

Until replaced by verified data:

- target gross margin: 55%;
- internal delivery cost: R4,000/day;
- client capacity available per accountable lead: 14 delivery-equivalent days/month;
- Care support and administration allowance: 0.25 day/month;
- estimated Care infrastructure/tooling allowance: R1,500/client/month;
- Managed Improvement delivery load: 2.75 days/month including governance and support;
- CI Partner delivery load: 6.50 days/month including governance and support;
- third-party usage remains billed separately.

Pricing formula:

```text
Minimum sustainable price
= direct monthly delivery cost ÷ (1 − target gross margin)
```

## 4. Illustrative tier economics

### Care

```text
0.25 day × R4,000             = R1,000
Infrastructure/tooling        = R1,500
Illustrative direct cost      = R2,500
Price required at 55% margin  = R5,556
Current public price          = R7,500
```

Initial conclusion: Care is commercially plausible if average support consumption remains controlled and material third-party costs continue to be billed separately.

### Current Improve

```text
2.75 days × R4,000            = R11,000
Infrastructure/tooling        = R1,500
Illustrative direct cost      = R12,500
Price required at 55% margin  = R27,778
Current public price          = R18,500
```

Initial conclusion: the current price cannot safely support the proposed Managed Improvement promise at the assumed cost and target margin. Either the price must rise, the scope must shrink, or the tier must remain a limited development-capacity product without outcome accountability.

Proposed test price: **from R28,500/month ex VAT**.

### Current Partner

```text
6.50 days × R4,000            = R26,000
Infrastructure/tooling        = R1,500
Illustrative direct cost      = R27,500
Price required at 55% margin  = R61,111
Current public price          = R38,500
```

Initial conclusion: the current price does not support genuine workstream partnership at the assumed effort. A public floor below approximately R61,500 produces less than the target margin under these assumptions.

Price required by the model: approximately **R61,500/month ex VAT**. The recommended test price is **from R62,500/month ex VAT**, subject to actual delivery costs.

## 5. Recommended commercial paths

### Option A — Protect the current prices by reducing the promise

- Care at R7,500;
- Improve at R18,500 as technical support plus two planned days, without monthly process analysis or benefits accountability;
- Partner at R38,500 as five planned development days plus quarterly planning, without workstream ownership.

Advantage: minimal price disruption.  
Disadvantage: does not deliver the new process-optimisation position.

### Option B — Replace the current higher tiers

- Care at R7,500;
- Managed Improvement from R28,500;
- Continuous Improvement Partner from R62,500;
- Outcome Partnership priced individually.

Advantage: simple, credible and aligned to the new proposition.  
Disadvantage: visible price increase and a larger enterprise buying decision.

### Option C — Keep technical tiers and add managed tiers

- Care at R7,500;
- Technical Improve at R18,500;
- Managed Improvement from R28,500;
- Technical Partner at R38,500;
- Continuous Improvement Partner from R62,500.

Advantage: existing clients retain familiar options.  
Disadvantage: too many tiers, harder selling and high risk of buyers choosing the cheapest superficially similar option.

### Recommendation

Adopt **Option B** for new clients. Grandfather existing signed agreements until renewal, then migrate them using actual consumption and value evidence.

## 6. Annual prepayment caution

A 10% annual prepayment discount reduces the proposed Managed Improvement price of R28,500 to an effective R25,650/month, below the illustrative 55% margin requirement.

Recommendation:

- retain 10% only for Care if the validated margin supports it;
- use 5% or no prepayment discount for labour-intensive managed tiers;
- treat improved cash flow as valuable, but do not allow it to erase delivery margin;
- continue annual escalation on the undiscounted list price.

## 7. Outcome fee model

Outcome Partnership pricing should contain four components:

```text
Monthly charge
= base operating fee
+ verified outcomes × unit fee
+ approved third-party usage
+ separately approved project work
```

The base operating fee must cover the minimum team, infrastructure, governance and compliance burden even when outcome volume temporarily falls.

The outcome component must define:

- qualifying event;
- source of truth;
- exclusions and reversals;
- reconciliation period;
- baseline or control group;
- seasonality;
- client-caused failure;
- maximum and minimum payment;
- dispute process;
- audit rights.

## 8. Data required to approve pricing

Collect the following for at least the preceding six months where available:

- salary and contractor cost by delivery role;
- employer costs and overhead allocation;
- actual productive delivery days per role;
- hosting, monitoring, backup and tooling cost per system;
- support time per client and severity;
- account-management and review time;
- unbilled rework;
- presales and proposal effort;
- leave and bench allowance;
- third-party licence consumption;
- current retainer revenue and actual effort;
- churn and renewal history.

## 9. Pilot validation design

Before public launch, test Managed Improvement with two or three existing system clients.

### Selection criteria

- functioning production system;
- engaged process owner;
- available transaction history;
- visible improvement backlog;
- moderate rather than crisis-level support demand;
- willingness to participate in monthly and quarterly reviews;
- permission to create an anonymised result story.

### Three-month test

Track:

- hours and days by activity type;
- meeting and preparation load;
- incident burden;
- number of improvements completed;
- number with measurable results;
- client participation;
- benefit delivered;
- delivery gross margin;
- renewal intention;
- willingness to pay at the proposed price.

### Approval thresholds

Before publishing the price, require:

- median capacity use within 10% of the planned tier allowance;
- no repeated dependence on unpaid extra capacity;
- positive contribution margin in every pilot account;
- portfolio gross margin on a credible route to the agreed target;
- at least one defensible quantified outcome;
- client confirmation that the operating cadence is valuable independently of feature output.

## 10. Founder decisions required before publication

1. Confirm the target gross margin.
2. Confirm current fully loaded delivery cost by role.
3. Choose the final CI Partner list-price format: fixed public price, from-price, or custom.
4. Confirm whether current clients will be grandfathered and for how long.
5. Approve the reduced annual-prepayment discount for managed tiers.
6. Confirm standard business hours and whether any existing agreement promises after-hours support.
7. Confirm the maximum number of managed accounts that may be sold before an additional lead is hired.
8. Approve the first two or three clients for the managed-improvement test.
