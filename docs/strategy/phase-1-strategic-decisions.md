# 2KO Systems — Phase 1 Strategic Decisions

Status: Working strategic baseline for Phase 2 commercial design  
Date: 12 September 2026

## 1. Executive decision

2KO Systems will be positioned as the **Systemise and Sustain arm of the 2KO Group**.

It will not present itself as a general software development agency or attempt to duplicate the complete training proposition of Six Sigma South Africa. Its role is to turn improved operational processes into dependable workflows, controls and systems, then remain involved through managed continuous improvement.

The primary positioning is:

> 2KO improves operational processes, automates the controls that should not depend on memory, and keeps the resulting system improving.

The recommended public-facing message is:

> **Improve the process. Automate what should never fail.**

Supporting explanation:

> 2KO identifies operational friction, improves the workflow and builds the systems that keep the gain from slipping backwards. When the constraint is capability, we train. When it is repetition, we automate. When it is both, we do both.

The recurring operating principle is:

> **Automate where the rules repeat. Train where judgement matters. Measure both.**

## 2. Purpose of the Systems site

The primary purpose of 2kosystems.com is to convert qualified operations buyers into a Process Review, Opportunity Audit or Proof-of-Value Pilot.

The site must help a buyer understand that:

1. 2KO starts with the operational problem, not a predetermined technology.
2. The recommended intervention may be process redesign, training, automation, a custom system, or a combination.
3. Every material intervention begins with a baseline and written success criteria.
4. Software is used to embed and sustain an improved process, not merely digitise the current one.
5. 2KO can remain accountable after launch through a managed improvement relationship.

Secondary purposes are to:

- qualify unsuitable work early;
- route training-led needs to Six Sigma South Africa;
- route programme and platform needs to Sigmafy;
- provide procurement confidence through transparent scope, ownership and commercial terms;
- retain focused landing pages for website leads without allowing that service to define the main brand.

## 3. Primary audience

### Primary client profile

Established South African and African organisations with operational workflows in which delay, variation, rework, poor visibility or missed controls create material cost or risk.

Primary sectors remain:

- mining and minerals;
- industrial and manufacturing;
- logistics and distribution;
- agriculture and agri-processing.

The proposition also serves construction and field services; energy, utilities and infrastructure; property, hospitality and facilities; banking, insurance and other financial services; healthcare and life sciences; retail, wholesale and multi-site operations; telecoms and technology operations; professional and business services; public services; education; and other process-heavy organisations. These are valid opportunities presented through a process-fit lens. They broaden the addressable market without displacing the four primary operating-sector examples until 2KO has sufficient systems-specific evidence in each adjacent sector.

### Primary buyers

- Chief Operating Officer;
- Operations Director or General Manager;
- Operational Excellence or Continuous Improvement leader;
- Transformation or Automation sponsor;
- IT leader sponsoring an operational system;
- Site, plant, distribution or functional manager with a measurable process problem.

### Primary buyer situations

The site will support three main starting points:

1. **The problem is visible.** A spreadsheet, approval chain, report or job-card process has clearly failed and the buyer wants it fixed.
2. **The symptoms are visible but the cause is unclear.** Cost, delay, exceptions or non-compliance are increasing, but the buyer cannot confidently name the constraint.
3. **An earlier improvement is decaying.** A better process was designed or people were trained, but the control still depends on memory, manual checking or disconnected tools.

## 4. The customer promise

The site will describe one continuous improvement loop:

1. **Diagnose** — identify the operational friction.
2. **Measure** — establish the performance baseline and cost.
3. **Improve** — remove waste, ambiguity and unnecessary handling.
4. **Train** — build capability where people and judgement remain essential.
5. **Automate** — remove repetitive handling and embed routine controls.
6. **Systemise** — establish a dependable operational record and workflow.
7. **Sustain** — monitor performance and run a continuing improvement cycle.

This model may be mapped to DMAIC, but it must be communicated as the client's improvement lifecycle rather than only as 2KO's software delivery process.

## 5. Brand architecture

| Brand | Defined role | Relationship to the client outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2KO Group | Operational excellence umbrella and group entry point | Connects the client to the appropriate intervention |
| Six Sigma South Africa | Training, certification and practitioner-led improvement | Builds capability and delivers improvement projects |
| 2KO Systems | Process automation, operational systems and managed improvement | Embeds the improved standard into daily work |
| Sigmafy | Improvement programme platform | Sustains projects, measures progress and supports scale |

Cross-brand language will be consistent:

> Diagnose. Train. Improve. Systemise. Sustain.

The Systems site may explain and route to training, but will not reproduce the training catalogue. The training site may identify opportunities for systemisation, but will not attempt to sell custom software in detail.

## 6. Treatment of the website service

Websites will remain a valid 2KO commercial service, but they will no longer receive equal prominence in the primary operational-systems navigation.

The working decision is:

- retain the existing website landing pages, pricing and search/advertising routes;
- remove Websites from the principal operational buyer journey;
- place it in secondary navigation or label it clearly as a separate digital service;
- do not show entry-level website prices beside enterprise operational-system pricing in the main Systems engagement ladder;
- evaluate a separate sub-brand or sub-site only after traffic, lead quality and revenue contribution have been reviewed.

This preserves the revenue stream without weakening the process-performance position.

## 7. Approved offer architecture

### Diagnostic entry points

#### Free Fit Call

A short qualification conversation used to identify the right paid starting point. It is not a consulting deliverable.

#### Half-Day Process Review

Purpose: identify the process in scope, visible failure points, likely impact and the appropriate next step.

Permitted outcomes are:

- improve the process;
- provide training;
- automate a bounded workflow;
- build or replace a system;
- investigate further through an audit;
- use an off-the-shelf product;
- do not build anything.

#### Process and Automation Opportunity Audit

This replaces the technology-presumptive framing of the Systems Opportunity Audit.

Required deliverables:

- current-state process map;
- baseline measures;
- three prioritised findings;
- benefit calculation with assumptions shown;
- effort, risk and confidence assessment;
- classification of each intervention as process, training, automation, system or combination;
- measurement and control plan;
- recommended pilot, where justified.

### Implementation offers

#### Workflow Automation Pilot

One bounded, measurable workflow delivered as a proof of value. Suitable examples include approval routing, escalation, reporting, document capture, reconciliation, job cards, spreadsheet replacement and exception handling.

#### Operational System Build

A phased custom system that embeds an improved operational process. It will be sold through the sequence:

> baseline → improved process → automated control → measurable result

#### Train, Improve and Embed Programme

A cross-group programme in which a training cohort completes a live improvement project and 2KO Systems embeds the resulting control plan into the operational workflow. Sigmafy or a managed retainer may then track whether the gain holds.

This is the recommended future flagship group offer, subject to joint commercial design with the training division.

## 8. Automation position

2KO will not sell generic AI transformation. Automation will be divided into two understandable categories.

### Rules-based automation

- approvals and routing;
- reminders and escalation;
- validation and error-proofing;
- reconciliation;
- scheduled reporting;
- system integrations;
- exception generation;
- audit trails.

### Intelligence-assisted automation

- document extraction;
- classification and triage;
- summarisation and drafting;
- knowledge copilots;
- anomaly identification;
- suggested responses;
- conversational agents where a conversation is the workstream.

Human judgement remains attached to consequential decisions. AI will be presented as a component of an improved workflow, not as the identity of the business.

## 9. Retainer architecture and boundaries

Retainers are not licences or discounted buckets of development time. They purchase an operating cadence, defined accountability and measurable improvement.

### Care — keep the system reliable

Scope:

- hosting and monitoring;
- automated backups;
- security patching;
- incident response against a stated SLA;
- corrective maintenance;
- system health reporting.

Boundary: Care does not include process analysis, feature development, training or improvement-roadmap ownership.

### Managed Improvement — keep one system-enabled process improving

Scope:

- one named system and its primary workflow;
- monthly performance scorecard;
- exception and trend analysis;
- prioritised improvement backlog;
- defined monthly change capacity;
- adoption and data-quality monitoring;
- quarterly benefits review;
- targeted refresher training where capability is the identified constraint.

Boundary: Managed Improvement does not constitute ownership of the client's wider operation, unlimited changes, major integrations or a multi-process transformation programme.

### Continuous Improvement Partner — jointly own one workstream roadmap

Scope:

- one clearly bounded operational workstream;
- named improvement lead;
- fortnightly operating review;
- agreed operational metrics;
- process, training and technology interventions;
- roadmap and backlog ownership;
- management reporting and benefits tracking;
- quarterly steering session;
- separately stated delivery capacity and response times.

Boundary: The client retains operational decision rights, management accountability and ownership of staff performance. Major builds and unrelated workstreams are separately scoped.

### Outcome Partnership — selected cases only

Commercial structure:

> Base fee plus a defined performance component.

Suitable outcomes must be independently measurable and substantially attributable to the intervention, such as completed bookings, collected balances, resolved exceptions, processing time or avoided manual cases.

Pure outcome-only pricing is not an approved default. It may be used only after a clean baseline, attribution rules, data access and client responsibilities are agreed in writing.

## 10. Retainer operating cadence

Every Managed Improvement or Continuous Improvement Partner account will run the same cycle:

1. measure current performance;
2. review exceptions and constraints;
3. select the highest-value improvement;
4. determine whether the intervention is process, training, system or combination;
5. implement the agreed change;
6. verify the result;
7. update the control plan and backlog;
8. report cumulative benefit.

Minimum monthly outputs:

- updated performance scorecard;
- prioritised backlog;
- change and decision record;
- measured result or explicit learning;
- next improvement commitment.

Minimum quarterly output:

- benefits-realisation review separating observed, calculated and client-reported results.

## 11. Capacity guardrails

Retainers will be capacity-limited and assigned to a named accountable lead.

Initial planning assumptions, to be validated during Phase 2:

- Care can scale through standardisation and monitoring automation, provided incident rates remain controlled.
- The preliminary Managed Improvement estimate was six to eight accounts per senior lead at two delivery days each. The fuller Phase 2 model, which includes governance, analysis and support, reduces the recommended initial cap to five accounts per lead.
- Continuous Improvement Partner should be capped at approximately two to three accounts per lead.
- A genuinely managed workstream may require one lead for only one or two concurrent clients.

The existing public Partner price must not be used to promise full workstream ownership until the delivery effort, support burden, process expertise and commercial margin have been modelled.

Every retainer agreement must specify:

- named system or workstream;
- baseline and agreed metrics;
- client owner;
- governance cadence;
- included delivery capacity;
- support response time;
- data access;
- exclusions;
- change approval process;
- treatment of unused capacity;
- definition and attribution of outcomes.

## 12. Claims and evidence rules

### Claims that may be made when company records support them

- 2KO was established in 1998.
- The group has served more than 1,000 organisations.
- 2KO provides training, process improvement and operational systems through specialist divisions.
- Project scope and pricing are agreed in writing.
- Clients own commissioned source code, documentation and data under the applicable contract.
- 2KO uses mainstream technology and avoids proprietary platform lock-in.
- 2KO may recommend that no custom system be built.

### Claims that require project-level evidence

- percentage cost reductions;
- cycle-time improvements;
- revenue or collection increases;
- reduced defects, rework or downtime;
- saved administrative hours;
- adoption, compliance or exception-resolution improvements;
- ROI multiples;
- sustained performance gains;
- production uptime or service-volume claims.

### Evidence standard

Every published result will state:

- the baseline period;
- the measurement period;
- the metric definition;
- material assumptions;
- whether the result is observed, calculated or client-reported;
- whether other interventions contributed;
- whether the client and process are named or anonymised.

Invented demonstrations may prove interaction and technical capability, but must not be presented as evidence of operational results.

Client logos will be labelled accurately. If the relationship was with another 2KO division, the site will say **organisations served across the 2KO Group** rather than implying that each logo represents a 2KO Systems implementation.

## 13. Language rules for the future site

Lead with:

- process performance;
- measurable operational problems;
- baseline and result;
- bottlenecks, variation, rework and exceptions;
- embedded controls;
- improvement that holds;
- the choice between process, training and technology interventions.

Avoid leading with:

- feature lists;
- generic digital transformation;
- generic AI claims;
- the assumption that every problem needs software;
- language that suggests automating the existing process without first challenging it;
- days of development as the main value of a retainer;
- unsupported performance claims.

The preferred CTA is:

> **Bring us the process that keeps going wrong.**

## 14. Phase 1 decisions and Phase 2 dependencies

### Decisions now established

- 2KO Systems is the Systemise and Sustain specialist within the group.
- Process optimisation is the organising idea; software is an intervention.
- Training is visible and routable but remains owned by the training division.
- Websites are removed from the primary operational buyer journey.
- Automation is process-led and divided into rules-based and intelligence-assisted work.
- Retainers are repositioned around reliability, managed improvement and workstream partnership.
- Outcome pricing is selective and hybrid by default.
- Measurable claims require a documented evidence standard.

### Inputs required during Phase 2

- current delivery headcount and realistic billable capacity;
- actual hosting, monitoring and support cost per live system;
- historic incident and support volume;
- gross-margin target by service;
- existing client appetite for each retainer level;
- two to four candidate projects with defensible before-and-after evidence;
- training-division availability for the Train, Improve and Embed offer;
- traffic, lead quality and revenue contribution from the website service;
- confirmation of group-level company, client and accreditation claims.

Phase 2 will convert these strategic decisions into priced service definitions, qualification rules, operating playbooks and sales-ready offer sheets.
